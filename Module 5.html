PART 1- I started of with doing some research on actuators and this is what I learnt;

Definition; An actuator is a component of a machine that produces force, torque, or displacement, when an electrical, pneumatic or hydraulic input is supplied to it in a system. The effect is usually produced in a controlled way. An actuator translates such an input signal into the required form of mechanical energy.

Types of Actuators by Energy Source:

  • Electric Actuators
    • Use electrical energy to generate motion; includes solenoids, electric motors (AC, DC, stepper), and piezoelectric types.
  • Pneumatic Actuators
    • Operate using compressed air, common in industrial automation due to speed, cost-effectiveness, and simplicity.
  • Hydraulic Actuators
    • Utilize pressurized fluid to deliver high force and precision—ideal for heavy-duty applications.
  • Mechanical Actuators
    • Rely on physical elements like screws, gears, cams, or levers to create motion; often manually powered and very reliable.

    Types of Motion:

  • Linear Actuators
    • Produce straight-line motion—typical forms include piston-based, diaphragm, and screw-driven systems.
  • Rotary Actuators
    • Generate circular or angular movement—for instance, rack-and-pinion mechanisms or vane-style hydraulic units.

    Common Applications:
    • Industrial Automation & Robotics- Positioning, conveyor control, valve actuation
    • Automotive- Seat adjustment, throttle control, braking systems
    • Aerospace & Defense- Flight control surfaces, landing gear, precise actuation
    • Medical Devices- Prosthetics, surgical tools, patient support systems
    • Smart Systems- Solar tracking, HVAC control, automated valves
